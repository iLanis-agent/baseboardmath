Boundary: arithmetic only Baseboard Math counts baseboard and trim with division and rounding-up: the pieces a run takes, the runs a piece yields, the nails along a run, and the cost. It uses one named constant: 12 inches in a foot. It applies the counting as written, so it has not seen your room and does not know your corners, your miters, your waste habits or your nailing practice, and it gives no verdict that an order is enough or that a job will fit. Every other number here is your own entry.

Starting examples: 126 ft of trim in 8 ft pieces, 5 runs of 36 inches from 96 inch pieces, nails every 16 inches with 2 a point, pieces at $7.25.

The 126 ft of trim in 8 ft pieces takes 16 pieces, leaving 2 ft.

The runs and nails truths

The 5 runs of 36 inches from 96 inch pieces take 3 pieces, 2 runs from each piece, leaving 108 inches. The 126 ft of trim at 16 inches takes 96 nailing points, 2 nails each, 192 nails in all.
